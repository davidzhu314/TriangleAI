- 2026-11-07 · Vaibhav · **5 mm-window turning, defined.** θ_w(s) is the angle between the chords p(s)−p(s−5 mm) and p(s+5 mm)−p(s). Θmax = max θ_w. ΘΣ = (1/w)∫θ_w ds + both end windows, which is the §4.2 sum of 5 mm-window angles. The full algorithm is in `tests/crosscheck/README.md`; the TypeScript in V2 follows it. Why: it is exact on arcs (θmax = w/R, ΘΣ = L/R) and conserves turning across straight-to-curve transitions. On a helix it differs from κL by the window's own smoothing (0.8° over two turns), so the helix fixture's closed form is (L/w)·θ_w.
- 2026-11-07 · Vaibhav · **Route-level turning.** Route Θmax and ΘΣ use the same 5 mm-window algorithm, run on the route's joined polyline: edges in travel order, reversed where walked backwards, with a shared junction point counted once. Windows therefore span branch points. Junction arc lengths are added to the 0.25 mm sample grid, so a corner's peak is exact wherever the grid falls. A corner of deflection (takeoff) angle δ between straight legs each ≥ 5 mm reads Θmax = ΘΣ = δ. Definition in `tests/crosscheck/README.md`; fixture `tests/fixtures/synthetic/junction/` (120° and 45° takeoffs). Why: per-edge turning alone misses the turn at a branch point, which is the turn a wire has to make.

- 2026-11-07 · Vaibhav · **Rail wire constants (illustrative).** Tip speed = push × 0.02 m/s (pull back with push < 0); roll = rotate × π rad/s. Wall-force proxy: F = 30 N·s/m × push speed × max(0, θ − 30°) / 60°, where θ is the angle between the tip's heading (its last 5 mm) and the vessel ahead (the next 5 mm); F = 30 N·s/m × push speed while the tip is stuck. force_spike fires when F rises through 0.5 N, and the event records that threshold so V5 scoring can own it. Wire body: one point per 2 mm, entry to tip, with a 0.3 mm sinusoidal offset (40 mm wavelength) fading to 0 over the last 10 mm, so the tip stays on the centerline; while prolapsed, a 4 mm bow over the last 30 mm. Constants in `client/src/wire/rail.ts`. Why: §5.3 names these behaviours without numbers; the simplest forms that grow as the spec says.
- 2026-11-07 · Vaibhav · **Rail branch entry as implemented.** At a branch point the candidates are the node's other edges. The tip direction is the heading over the last 5 mm, bent by the preset's tip angle and rolled by φ from a fixed reference (world +Z made perpendicular to the heading, or +X when the heading is within ~25° of ±Z). Each candidate's direction is its first 5 mm. The tip enters the candidate closest to the tip direction if it is within 25°. Otherwise it "continues along the parent", meaning the candidate closest to the incoming heading (the straightest way). Entering an edge that is not in the plan records wrong_branch, with expectedEdge the plan's edge at the point the walk left the plan. Pulling back past a branch point backs out of that edge. Rail's s is arc length along the walked path, which equals the plan's s while on plan.
- 2026-11-07 · Vaibhav · **Rail prolapse as implemented.** "A turn sharper than the preset allows" is the 5 mm-window turn the optimizer checks: the deflection into the next edge at a branch point, or a window inside an edge, against the preset's max turn (110/80/40°). Pushing into one stops the tip there and records prolapse at once. "Tip against a wall" means pushing without moving, at a dead end that is not the target. After more than 1 s of that, the wire prolapses. There is one prolapse event per episode, the tip holds while bowed, and pulling back clears it. So a frontier route never prolapses: its turns all pass the same check.
- 2026-11-07 · Vaibhav · **Diamond fixture, route B takeoff 30° → 38°.** Under the rail's 25° branch-entry rule, Standard J (60° tip) cannot point into a 30° branch, so the V3 frontier route J-B was undrivable. At 38° both Soft angled (45°) and Standard J (60°) are within 25°. The frontier is unchanged in shape (soft-A, soft-B, J-B), and the V3 tests pass as before. The general gap is a finding, not fixed here: V3 feasibility checks turns but not whether a preset can point into a branch. A route whose takeoff is more than 25° from a preset's tip angle, and is not the straightest way on, can be on the frontier yet undrivable in rail. Closing it in the optimizer would need a new violation kind (a contract change), so it waits for a request.
- 2026-11-07 · Vaibhav · **Gentleness reference without recorded runs.** §6.2 compares force with "the frontier route's recorded values". No real recorded runs exist and a stand-in may not feed the app, so the reference is computed: the rail autopilot drives the frontier route that ranks best at the player's weights, and its peak force (N) and cumulative force (Σ F·Δt, N·s) are the reference. With no frontier (no safe route) the autopilot drives the player's own planned path. It is deterministic and needs no new data. It is a rail-proxy reference, so a physics-mode drive is compared with rail-proxy forces until real runs exist. Illustrative.
- 2026-11-07 · Vaibhav · **Gentleness and radiation curves (illustrative).** Gentleness: for peak and for cumulative force separately, 100 at or below the reference, falling linearly to 0 at reference + 2 × max(reference, floor), floors 0.1 N and 0.1 N·s; the score is the mean of the two. Radiation: fluoro seconds = samples with fluoro on ÷ sample rate; 100 at or below the route's estFluoroS, falling linearly to 0 at 2 × estFluoroS (any fluoro scores 0 if the estimate is 0). Constants in `client/src/score/constants.ts`. Why: §6.2 gives the anchor points only; straight lines are the simplest shape through them.
- 2026-11-07 · Vaibhav · **Plan score as implemented.** A cap applies as score = min(cap, 100·(1 − min(1, R/Rmax))); R = S(player) − min S over the frontier routes; Rmax = max S over feasible candidates − that same min; Rmax = 0 gives the regret part 100. S is V3's normalised S (§4.8), ranges from the feasible candidates. The player's route is found by id (`entry|preset|path`) among the candidates; if absent, it is measured with `measureRoute` and checked the same way. "No safe route": plan 0, decided by 'feasible', violations = the route's own, or the optimizer's reasons if it has none. The safer route (heavy cap) and the dominating route (frontier cap) are the best-ranked frontier routes at the player's weights that qualify. The top contributor is the measure with the largest w_k·(ĉ_k(player) − ĉ_k(best)), reported only when that is above 0.
- 2026-11-07 · Vaibhav · **Stars and radiation budget as implemented.** Not reaching the target gives 0 stars; both scores are still computed and shown.
